Research

Doctoral thesis

Debt Maturity and Firm Value in Malaysian Listed Firms The cost-of-capital channel and the conditional role of financial distress risk

Firms decide how much to borrow, and also for how long. My thesis asks whether that second choice changes what a firm is worth. It treats the cost of capital as the channel between debt maturity and firm value, and financial distress risk as the condition that alters it.

The work draws on trade-off, agency cost and debt maturity theory, and tests them on panel data for non-financial firms listed in Malaysia.

Interests

Capital structure, debt maturity, cost of capital, financial distress and firm value in emerging markets. Alongside these: corporate governance and financing decisions, asset pricing in thin and frontier markets, forensic analytics and data integrity, and professional accounting education.
